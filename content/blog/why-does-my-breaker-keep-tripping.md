A breaker that keeps tripping is doing its job — it's cutting power because the circuit is drawing more current than it's rated for, or because there's a fault. If it trips once and resets fine, it was probably a momentary overload. If it trips again within seconds, or won't reset at all, stop resetting it: that pattern means a short or a ground fault, and forcing it back on defeats the one device protecting your house from an electrical fire.

Here's how to think it through.

## First, which kind of trip is it?

**Overload.** The circuit is fine, you just asked too much of it. A space heater and a hair dryer on the same bathroom circuit. A microwave and a toaster on one kitchen counter circuit. The breaker trips after a minute or two of heavy load, and once you unplug something and reset it, it holds. This is annoying but not dangerous — the system worked. The fix is usually [a dedicated circuit or two](/electrical-outlet-services/) so the room isn't fighting over one 20-amp line.

**Short circuit.** A hot wire is touching a neutral or a ground somewhere — in a device, a damaged cord, or the wall. The breaker trips *instantly*, every time, often with a pop. It will not reset, or it resets and trips again in seconds. This one needs [an electrician to trace it](/electrical-troubleshooting/) before the power goes back on.

**Ground fault.** Current is leaking to ground — through moisture, a failing appliance, or damaged insulation. GFCI breakers and outlets are built to catch this because it's the fault that shocks people. Common in bathrooms, kitchens, garages, and outdoor circuits.

**A failing breaker.** Breakers wear out, especially after they've tripped on a real fault a few times. If one circuit trips with nothing unusual plugged in and everything downstream checks out, the breaker itself may be weak. This is more common — and more concerning — in Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels.

## What you can safely check yourself

- **Unplug everything on the circuit**, reset the breaker, and plug things back in one at a time. If it trips when a specific device goes in, that device is the problem.
- **Check for tripped GFCIs** in other rooms. A dead outlet in the bedroom is sometimes a GFCI tripped in the bathroom or garage on the same circuit.
- **Look and smell at the panel.** The panel should be cool, quiet, and free of any burning smell. If it's warm, buzzing, or smells like hot plastic, that's not a DIY situation — [call us](/emergency-electrical-repairs-parker-co/).
- **Note the pattern.** When does it trip — always at the same time of day, when the AC kicks on, when it rains? That pattern is half the diagnosis.

Do **not** replace a breaker with a higher-amperage one to stop the tripping. The breaker is sized to the wire. A bigger breaker lets the wire overheat before it trips, which is exactly how circuits start fires.

## When to stop and call

Call an electrician the same day if:

- The breaker won't reset, or trips again within seconds
- You see scorch marks, melted plastic, or discolouration at the breaker or any outlet
- The panel is warm to the touch, buzzing, or smells hot
- The tripping started after water got into a wall, ceiling, or the panel
- You have a Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) or Zinsco panel — these have a documented history of breakers that don't trip when they should, which is worse than one that trips too often
