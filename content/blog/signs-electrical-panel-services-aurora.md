Your electrical panel is the one part of the system every circuit runs through. When it is undersized, overloaded, or just old, you get symptoms all over the house that look like separate problems. [Aurora](/electricians/aurora-co/) has a wide range of housing stock, from 1960s and 70s homes with original equipment to newer builds already 20-plus years old, and the panel is where age shows up first. Here is what to watch for.

## Breakers that trip repeatedly

A breaker doing its job occasionally is fine. A breaker that trips every time two normal appliances run together is telling you the circuit or the panel is overloaded. Resetting it over and over is not a fix, and it is not safe to keep forcing it. Sometimes the answer is rebalancing circuits; sometimes it is more capacity. A [load assessment](/electrical-panel-services/) tells you which.

## A fuse box instead of breakers

Fuses work, but the panels are almost always undersized for a modern house, and fuses get replaced with the wrong amperage over the years, which defeats the protection. If you still have a fuse box, an upgrade is worth planning.

## A Federal Pacific or Zinsco label

Homes in Aurora built between about 1960 and 1983 sometimes have a Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) or Zinsco panel. Both brands have a documented history of breakers that fail to trip on a fault, which is the one thing a breaker absolutely has to do. Most electricians, and many insurers, recommend replacing them. If you see either name on your panel, get it looked at.

## Heat, rust, buzzing, or a burning smell

Open the panel door and put a hand near the cover. It should be cool, silent, and free of any smell. Warmth, a buzzing sound, rust streaks, or the smell of hot plastic all mean a connection is arcing behind the deadfront or water has gotten in. That is a same-week call, not a someday call.

## 100-amp service and a growing list of loads

A house built for a furnace, a water heater, and some lights is now running central AC, an EV charger, a hot tub, and an electric range. If you are adding any of those, find out whether the service can take it before you buy the equipment. Many Aurora homes need to move from 100 to 200-amp service once the loads add up.

## Repair or replace?

Not every panel problem is a replacement. A loose lug, a failed breaker, or a labeling job are repairs. A fuse box, a recalled panel brand, water damage, or genuinely maxed-out capacity are replacements.
