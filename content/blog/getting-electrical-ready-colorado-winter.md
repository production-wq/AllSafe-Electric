Before the first real storm, three things are worth ten minutes each: check that your outdoor circuits are weather-sealed and GFCI-protected, make sure your panel isn't already maxed out before you add winter loads, and decide now, not during an outage. Whether a standby generator is something you want. Everything else is a bonus.

Here's the short version.

## Outdoor circuits and holiday lighting

- **Test every exterior GFCI outlet.** Push the test button; it should cut power. Push reset; it should restore it. If it doesn't do both, it's failed and needs replacing before it's under a foot of snow.
- **Check the covers.** Exterior outlets need an "in-use" (bubble) cover that closes over a plugged-in cord. A flat flip-lid that won't shut over your holiday-light plug isn't weatherproof.
- **Don't run holiday lights off a bathroom GFCI through a cracked window.** It trips, it's a pinch point for cold air, and it's a nuisance every year. A switched, weatherproof [exterior lighting circuit](/outdoor-lighting/) with a timer solves it permanently and is a quick fall job, book it before December.

## The space-heater math

Portable space heaters are the single biggest winter overload we get called about. A 1,500-watt heater pulls about 12.5 amps. Most of a 15-amp circuit on its own. Put it on a circuit that's already running a TV, a lamp, and a phone charger, and the breaker trips. Put two heaters on one circuit and you're overloading the wire.

Rules that prevent a call to us:

- One space heater per circuit, plugged **directly into the wall**, never a power strip or extension cord
- If a room needs supplemental heat every winter, that's a sign it wants [a dedicated circuit](/electrical-outlet-services/) or a proper baseboard/wall heater, not a portable unit run daily

## Is your panel ready for winter loads?

If you heat with a heat pump or electric furnace, winter is when your [panel](/electrical-panel-services/) works hardest. Before the cold sets in:

- Open the panel and check that nothing is warm, discoloured, or buzzing
- Make sure the panel schedule is legible. During an outage or a fault, you want to find the right breaker fast
- If breakers have started tripping in the last year with nothing new added, get it looked at now rather than at 10pm in a snowstorm

## Standby generators. Decide before you need one

Front Range wind events and ice storms put parts of Douglas County dark for hours to days. If you're on a well or septic pump, work from home, or have someone in the house on powered medical equipment, an [automatic standby generator](/generator-installation/) turns a multi-day outage into a non-event. It sits on a pad beside the house, runs on natural gas or propane, and starts itself within seconds.

The reason to decide in the fall: install lead times stretch out once the first big outage hits and everyone calls at once. A load assessment now means you're ready before the season, not on a waiting list during it.

## The five-minute list

1. Test every outdoor GFCI (test *and* reset)
2. Confirm exterior outlet covers close over a plugged-in cord
3. One space heater per circuit, straight into the wall
4. Look and listen at the panel, cool, quiet, labelled
5. If you've thought "we should get a generator" two winters running, get the assessment done
