If your home was built between about 1960 and 1983 and still has its original panel, there's a real chance it's a Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) or a Zinsco. Both brands have a documented history of breakers that fail to trip on an overload or a short. Meaning the circuit keeps drawing current while the wire overheats. The recommended fix for both is replacement, and most insurers and home inspectors agree.

You can usually identify one yourself in a couple of minutes.

## Identifying a Federal Pacific panel

- Open the panel door. Look for the name **Federal Pacific Electric**, **FPE**, or the **Stab-Lok** label on the panel or the breakers.
- The breaker handles are often **thin and close together**, and many have a distinctive **red stripe or red tip** when on.
- The panel label may read **"Federal Pacific Electric Co."** near the top.

Testing by UL and independent labs found a significant share of Stab-Lok breakers didn't trip within a safe margin, or at all, under fault conditions. That's the concern: not that the panel will fail today, but that the protection you're counting on may not be there when a fault happens.

## Identifying a Zinsco (or GTE-Sylvania) panel

- Look for **Zinsco**, **GTE-Sylvania**, or **Sylvania-Zinsco** on the panel label or breakers.
- The breakers are often **multi-coloured**. Blue, green, red, bronze, which is unusual and a good tell.
- The bus bar (the metal the breakers clip onto) is aluminium and prone to corrosion and overheating, which can weld a breaker to the bus so it can't trip.

## What to do if you have one

**Don't panic, and don't ignore it.** These panels can run for years without incident. But the safe move is to plan a replacement rather than wait for a symptom.

1. **Get it inspected.** A [licensed electrical inspection](/home-electrical-safety-inspections/) confirms the brand, checks the bus and breakers for heat damage and corrosion, and documents the grounding, which you'll want for the panel work and for your insurer.
2. **Replace the panel.** A [panel replacement](/electrical-panel-services/) swaps the whole unit for a modern one with breakers that trip reliably, proper grounding and bonding, a surge-protection slot, and a clean labelled schedule. It's usually a one-day job.
3. **Tell your insurer.** Some Colorado carriers won't write or renew a policy on a home with an active FPE or Zinsco panel. Replacing it can remove that problem and sometimes lowers the premium.
