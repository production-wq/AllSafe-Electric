A lot of Parker homeowners assume the electrical system got a thorough check during their home inspection. It did not. A general home inspector does a visual, non-invasive pass: they look at fixtures, trip-test a few outlets, and note anything obvious. They do not open the panel, and they do not run the system under load. A dedicated [electrical safety inspection](/home-electrical-safety-inspections/) is a licensed electrician doing exactly those things, and it is worth doing before you buy a home, on any house over 20 years old, or after a storm or a surge.

## The service entrance and meter

We start outside, at the point where the utility's power enters the house: the mast, the meter base, the service conductors, and the clearances. This is where we catch a service that is undersized for the house, or damage that the homeowner never sees.

## The main panel

We remove the deadfront cover and look inside. That means checking:

- The main breaker rating against the size of the incoming conductors
- The bus bars for pitting, corrosion, or heat discoloration
- Every breaker connection, torqued to spec with a calibrated screwdriver
- Double-tapped breakers, where a previous installer put two wires under one lug
- The panel brand, because a Federal Pacific or Zinsco means a different conversation

Loose connections generate heat, and heat is what damages breakers and starts fires behind the wall. This is the single most valuable part of the inspection and it is the part a home inspector never does.

## Grounding and bonding

We test the grounding electrode system rather than just looking at it. Proper grounding is what makes every other safety device in the house work, and it is commonly incomplete in older homes or after a remodel.

## GFCI and AFCI protection

We test the ground-fault protection in the kitchen, bathrooms, garage, and outdoors, and the arc-fault protection where it exists. We note where code now requires it and it is missing.

## A sample of outlets, switches, and wiring

We check a representative set of devices for correct wiring, polarity, and grounding, and we look at visible wiring in the attic, basement, and crawl space for aluminium branch circuits, damaged runs, or old cloth-covered wire.

## What you get

A written report the same day or the next morning, with photos, and every item rated one of three ways: safe, monitor, or address now. If you want the flagged items fixed, that is a separate quote and there is no obligation. You can take the report to another contractor, a lender, or an insurer.

The inspection runs about an hour for a typical Douglas County home. Book one before you are under contract on a house, while you still have room to negotiate.
